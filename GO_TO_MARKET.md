# Go-to-market

## Promotion thesis

Broad “life organizer” marketing is too weak. Promotion should start where intent and anxiety already exist: relocation, document replacement, foreigner registration, property taxes, and dependant paperwork.

## 90-day launch

### Weeks 1–3 — concierge validation

- Interview 20 movers, cross-border families, and property/vehicle owners.
- Build plans with them manually and identify the 20 most repeated obligations.
- Measure whether they value maintained applicability or only reminders.
- Pre-sell founding annual plans.
- Recruit two catalog reviewers: one Russia-focused and one Uzbekistan-focused.

### Weeks 4–7 — closed beta

- Russia and Uzbekistan packs.
- Russian and English first; Uzbek Latin after editorial review.
- 50–100 households from 3–5 focused Telegram communities.
- Concierge onboarding and a visible “does not apply / outdated” feedback control.
- Two relocation or diaspora partners.

### Weeks 8–12 — public launch

- Publish 15–25 source-backed high-intent pages.
- Launch paid annual and Family offers.
- Test the six-month Move Pack.
- Publish outcome-based testimonials only after users experience a real renewal or deadline.

## Channels

### Search

Create useful pages around exact jobs:

- passport replacement and validity;
- residence and foreigner-registration reviews;
- property and vehicle tax notices;
- child and dependant documents;
- moving Russia → Uzbekistan and Uzbekistan → Russia;
- self-employed recurring reviews.

Each page must show audience, scope, official source, last-reviewed date, known exceptions, and an “Add to my plan” action. Do not mass-produce thin legal-adjacent pages.

### Telegram and diaspora communities

Start with practical checklist posts and office-hours sessions, not display ads. Give community partners a maintained pack link and a referral code. Recruit beta households from narrow relocation and expatriate groups.

### Experts and creators

Work with relocation advisers, immigration lawyers, cross-border tax educators, property/vehicle specialists, and Russian/Uzbek diaspora creators. Ask them to review scope and distribute a pack—not to endorse completeness.

### Partnerships

Prioritize relocation agencies, universities, global-mobility employers, banks serving migrants, insurers, and property managers. Offer co-branded pack links and aggregate usage reporting.

### Product-led referral

- Invite a family member to share responsibility.
- Share a country-rules checklist without personal dates.
- Give both users one month of Plus when the invitee confirms three obligations.
- Generate a printable moving checklist without identifiers.

## Landing page

1. Lead with a multi-country household scenario.
2. Prove catalog coverage, sources, version, and review dates.
3. Show the people → countries → possessions → obligations model.
4. Provide a useful preview before sign-in.
5. Explain the privacy boundary and user confirmation.
6. Compare Free, Plus, Family, and Move Pack.
7. Offer Google sign-in plus email or generic OIDC fallback.
8. Answer coverage, accuracy, reminder, calendar, deletion, and rule-change questions.

## Metrics

- Landing → start a pack: 12%+ hypothesis.
- Onboarding completion: 60%+.
- Confirm at least three obligations: 45%+.
- Calendar export/connect: 35%+.
- Free → paid in 30 days: 4–7%.
- Annual share: 60%+.
- Monthly paid churn: under 4%.
- Family invite acceptance: 30%+.
- Catalog acceptance/dismissal and outdated reports by jurisdiction.

Do not optimize daily active use. NextDue should be low-frequency and high-trust.
